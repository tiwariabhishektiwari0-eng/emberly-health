Clinical medical coding is under more intense scrutiny than ever before. With commercial payers expanding their use of artificial intelligence and machine-learning claim scrubbers, even minor discrepancies in modifier assignment or clinical documentation specificity trigger automated denials and post-payment audits.

To protect your practice's earned revenue and maintain bulletproof compliance, use this **10-point coding accuracy checklist** before locking charges or releasing electronic billing batches.

---

### 1. Verification of Highest ICD-10-CM Specificity
- [ ] Are all diagnosis codes coded to their maximum number of characters (4th, 5th, 6th, or 7th digit)?
- [ ] Have "unspecified" codes (ending in .9) been replaced with specific anatomical site or etiology wherever documented?
- [ ] Are laterality indicators (1 for right, 2 for left, 3 for bilateral) verified against clinical exam notes?

### 2. Primary Diagnosis Justification (CPT Linking)
- [ ] Does the primary diagnosis code directly establish the medical necessity of the primary CPT procedure code billed?
- [ ] Are secondary chronic conditions (hypertension, diabetes, obesity) linked only when they actively influenced medical decision making during the encounter?

### 3. Evaluation & Management (E/M) Guidelines Alignment
- [ ] Does the visit note fulfill current AMA 2021/2023 documentation guidelines based on either:
  - **Medical Decision Making (MDM)**: Number and complexity of problems addressed, amount/complexity of data reviewed, and risk of complications/morbidity; OR
  - **Total Time**: Exact encounter minutes documented with specific face-to-face and non-face-to-face provider activities itemized?

### 4. Appropriate Modifier Usage
- [ ] **Modifier -25**: Is there clear, separate documentation showing a significant, separately identifiable E/M service on the same date as a minor surgical procedure?
- [ ] **Modifier -59 / -X{EPSU}**: Is there explicit chart evidence of a distinct procedural session, different anatomical site, separate incision, or independent organ system?
- [ ] **Modifier -26 / -TC**: Has the professional vs. technical component been appropriately assigned based on equipment ownership and facility setting?

### 5. Surgical Global Period Checks
- [ ] Is an E/M visit within a 10-day or 90-day surgical global period billed only if it is for a condition unrelated to the original surgery (using modifier -24)?
- [ ] If an unplanned return to the operating room occurred during the global period, is modifier -78 attached?
- [ ] If a planned staged procedure was performed, is modifier -58 documented?

### 6. Correct Place of Service (POS) Codes
- [ ] Does the POS code match the exact setting where the service occurred (e.g., POS 11 for Office, POS 22 for On-Campus Outpatient Hospital, POS 24 for Ambulatory Surgery Center)?
- [ ] For telehealth encounters, is POS 02 (Telehealth outside patient home) or POS 10 (Telehealth in patient home) properly selected with applicable modifiers (-95)?

### 7. National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) PTP Edits
- [ ] Have the billed procedure combinations been scrubbed against the most current quarterly CMS NCCI Procedure-to-Procedure (PTP) edit files?
- [ ] Does an unbundling modifier have legitimate clinical documentation substantiating the unbundling exception?

### 8. Drug & Biological Wastage Tracking (JW & JZ Modifiers)
- [ ] For single-dose vial drugs, is the discarded amount reported on a separate line item with the **-JW modifier**?
- [ ] If no drug was wasted from a single-dose vial, is the **-JZ modifier** correctly appended as required by CMS?

### 9. Timed Unit Calculations
- [ ] For physical therapy, psychotherapy, and anesthesia services, do the billed units align strictly with documentation guidelines (e.g. CMS 8-Minute Rule for physical medicine codes 97110, 97140)?
- [ ] Are start and stop times clearly noted in the clinical chart?

### 10. Provider Credentialing & Signature Integrity
- [ ] Is the rendering provider's NPI active and properly linked to the billing group's Tax ID?
- [ ] Has the supervising physician signed off on all mid-level or resident encounters meeting incident-to or split/shared criteria?

### Maintaining Institutional Quality

Implementing this checklist requires continuous collaboration between practice physicians, clinical nurses, and professional coders. When practices adopt dual-layer QA audits, first-pass clean claim acceptance rates routinely exceed **98%**, accelerating practice cash flow and eliminating audit anxiety.
